# Data statement

Following Bender and Friedman. Written for the released public partition.

## 1. Curation rationale

Professional finance examinations define, through externally published curricula, what a competent
practitioner is expected to know at each stage. Building a benchmark around those curricula makes
model comparison interpretable against professional expectations rather than against task labels
invented for the benchmark. Items were selected to preserve all five stages of the CFA and FRM
programs under one evaluation protocol, which no prior English benchmark does.

## 2. Language variety

English, professional and technical register. The items are written for an international
candidate audience and follow examination-body style rather than any single national variety.

Two documented exceptions inherited from the source material:

* 105 of 5,110 items (2.1 percent) carry a short Chinese case-analysis prefix
  at the start of the stem.
* 364 of 5,110 items (7.1 percent) carry a reference rationale written in
  Chinese. Rationales are metadata and are not model input in any experiment reported.

## 3. Speaker demographic

Not applicable in the usual sense. Items are professionally authored examination questions, not
elicited speech or user-generated text. Authorship sits with the preparatory-material publishers.

## 4. Annotator demographic

Four finance-qualified reviewers: a lead curator who is an author and coordinated the process, and
three external reviewers who are not authors and were paid above the statutory minimum hourly wage
of their jurisdiction. Each reviewer's qualification was matched to the examination stages they
were assigned, and all four consented to the use of their annotations. Every retained record passed a
stage-specific primary review and then a second full review by the lead curator, who integrated
corrections and applied deterministic checks for schema validity, identifier uniqueness,
answer-option mapping and content duplication. Further demographic detail is withheld for
anonymous review.

## 5. Speech situation

Written, asynchronous, edited. Items are composed for timed examination practice and carry the
conventions of that setting: context-complete stems where possible, deliberately plausible
distractors, and a fixed option count of three for CFA and four for FRM.

## 6. Text characteristics

Multiple-choice items across investment analysis, portfolio management, financial reporting,
quantitative methods, derivatives, ethics, and the risk-management topics of the FRM program.

| Stage | Items |
|---|---:|
| CFA Level I | 3,109 |
| CFA Level II | 1,030 |
| CFA Level III | 179 |
| FRM Part I | 516 |
| FRM Part II | 276 |

1,704 items (33.3 percent) refer to background the release does not carry,
almost always a table or a shared case vignette that existed as an image in the source. Those
items are flagged individually rather than silently retained, and the paper reports its
conclusion-bearing comparisons both with and without them.

## 7. Provenance

CFA and FRM aligned preparatory materials available for academic research. Official CFA Institute
and GARP examination content is excluded. Deduplication was global across the corpus before
partitioning, and the paper reports the counts removed at each step.

## 8. Known limitations

Accuracy on these items is not a competence assessment and must not be read as evidence that a
system is fit for professional financial practice. The benchmark measures examination-style
reasoning under a fixed protocol, nothing more.
